Is It That Deep?

Error-Correcting Codes, Macrophage Polarisation & Applied Biology in Civic Geography

Fair question: it’s a boarded-up corner pub and a fire-damaged warehouse beneath a railway viaduct in Watford—is it really that deep? Probably not, if you only look at bricks and mortar. And also, yes—because chronic urban neglect is a systems failure, and living systems have spent millions of years perfecting how to detect errors, stop the rot, and remodel damaged tissue at organism scale.

Error-Correcting Code in the Fabric of Things

01 // PROF. S. JAMES GATES JR. & CIVIC SIGNAL

In 2008, theoretical physicist Prof. S. James Gates Jr. and his collaborators (Doran, Faux, Hübsch, Iga, and Landweber) showed something genuinely startling while classifying the equations of supersymmetry using graphical diagrams they named adinkras (after the West African Adinkra visual symbols). Embedded inside those equations were the exact mathematical structures of doubly-even, self-dual linear binary error-correcting block codes—the same family of codes that prevents digital transmissions from degrading into noise.

You do not have to leap into sci-fi simulation theories to take a practical lesson from this. Whether in fundamental physics, cellular DNA repair, or a high street, resilient systems do not stay intact because nothing ever goes wrong. They stay intact because error-correction is built into the loop: when a bit flips or a corner starts to rot, a feedback mechanism detects the fault and restores coherence before the structure collapses.

When 317–319 Lower High Street and Chalk Hill sit behind Heras fencing for nearly a decade, the civic error-correcting code has been switched off. Action-research is how a neighbourhood turns that feedback loop back on.

Why This Metaphor Works

Noise vs. Signal: Speculative planning portals and nine years of “looking at the future use” introduce noise—disconnecting a building’s paper status from its physical reality.

The Civic Checksum: A dated Section 215 Evidence Pack, a 3D LiDAR scan, and a public regional log act like a parity check. They verify ground truth against paper claims and force the system to reconcile the error.

Human Activity as Macrophage Polarisation

02 // M1 CLEARANCE ↔ M2 REPAIR

In immunology, macrophages are the versatile first-responders of tissue homeostasis. Depending on the chemical signals in their microenvironment, the very same cell polarises into distinct functional states—broadly mapped along an M1 (pro-inflammatory / clearance) to M2 (pro-resolving / repair) spectrum. Looking at civic activity through macrophage polarisation clarifies why local campaigns so often stall—and how to move past the deadlock.

M1 Phenotype // Pro-Inflammatory & Clearance

Naming the Damage & Stopping the Rot

Triggered by injury or pathogens, M1 macrophages sound the alarm, clear necrotic debris, and stop infection from spreading.

  • Civic equivalent: Breaking silence, documenting deterioration, challenging nine years of stalled land, and pushing for a Section 215 Notice.
  • The failure mode: If a community stays locked in M1 forever—pure outrage, petitioning, and venting without a build plan—you get chronic inflammation and burnout, never new tissue.
M2 Phenotype // Pro-Resolving & Tissue Repair

Creating Starting Conditions for Growth

Once the acute threat is contained, macrophages switch to M2—dampening inflammation, secreting growth factors, and scaffolding new tissue.

  • Civic equivalent: Proposing an insured meanwhile-use licence, laying down the Tier 1 makerspace pilot, installing modular living walls, and stewarding pioneer ecology.
  • The failure mode: Jumping straight to M2 without M1 accountability leaves you politely asking permission while the roof keeps leaking and the rot continues underneath.
The Polarisation Switch: Healthy healing depends on timing the switch from M1 to M2. That is why this project pairs an enforcement tool (Site A: Section 215 Evidence Pack) directly with a constructive build offer (Tier 1 Makerspace & Biodiversity Net Gain Corridor)—so the neighbourhood can clear the hazard and immediately seed the repair.

The Four Stages of Healing at Organism Scale

03 // APPLIED BIOLOGY IN CIVIC GEOGRAPHY

Biological wound repair unfolds in four overlapping phases—Hemostasis, Inflammation, Proliferation, and Remodelling. Mapped onto the Bushey Arches corridor, these four stages give us a grounded operational sequence for action-research at an ecological-organism scale:

Stage 01 // Hemostasis

Stopping the Rot

In biology, the immediate priority is vasoconstriction and clotting—stopping the bleed and sealing the wound so the system does not keep losing integrity.

Bushey Arches Parallel: Serving the Section 215 Notice, securing broken ground-floor glazing, repairing the ripped-off drainpipe, halting water ingress, and ending fly-tipping and arson exposure at 317–319 Lower High Street and the adjacent warehouse.
Stage 02 // Inflammation (M1 Phase)

Clearing Debris & Establishing Ground Truth

Immune cells flood the site, clear dead matter, and map the boundary between damaged and healthy tissue.

Bushey Arches Parallel: Breaking public silence, running dated site logs and 3D LiDAR scans, clearing dumped waste (just as volunteers cleared Cody Dock), and making the gap between paper approvals (26/00641/CTR) and street reality impossible to ignore.
Stage 03 // Proliferation (M2 Transition)

Creating Starting Conditions for Growth

Fibroblasts lay down temporary extracellular matrix, new blood vessels sprout, and pioneer cells bridge the gap.

Bushey Arches Parallel: Activating the lean £2,500–£5,000 Tier 1 Pilot—an insured meanwhile-use makerspace, repair benches, modular living walls, and studying Site B’s pyrophilous fungi as living proof that pioneer biology is already scaffolding the corridor.
Stage 04 // Remodelling & Maturation

Ecological-Organism Scale Repair

Temporary scar matrix is gradually replaced by organised, load-bearing tissue aligned with the real forces acting on the body.

Bushey Arches Parallel: Converting temporary meanwhile-use momentum into permanent civic infrastructure—following the 30-mile benchmarks (Skip Garden’s 999-year lease, The Hat District, Camley Street) to anchor a lasting workshop and Biodiversity Net Gain corridor linking Bushey Arches to Oxhey Park Extension.

A Curious Case of Applied Biology in Civic Geography

04 // ACTION-RESEARCH IN PRACTICE

When psychologist Kurt Lewin coined action-research in 1946, he described it as a spiral of steps—planning, acting, observing, and reflecting—done inside a live social system rather than from a detached desk. Treating a neglected railway-viaduct corridor like a living wound takes that spiral seriously. It asks what happens when you stop treating a town gateway as an inert real-estate spreadsheet and start treating it as an ecological organism capable of self-correction, polarisation, and repair.

So—is it that deep? Only if we actually test it on the ground.
